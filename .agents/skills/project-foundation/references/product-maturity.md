# Develop an open product idea

Use for a new product, uncertain value proposition or materially changing ambition. A bounded fix or an already accepted feature does not reopen product discovery. Depth follows unresolved decisions and their consequences, not document length or a fixed number of questions.

## Preserve ambition before selecting a slice

Distinguish the intended product, the immediate deliverable and the evidence that deliverable can establish. An interview demonstration, an interaction prototype, a pilot with actual users and a commercial product have different acceptance bars. A request for a local prototype does not silently remove the wider vision, required actors or guarantees. Reuse the user's existing context and ask only where this distinction changes the next commitment.

Map the relevant actors, responsibilities, current journeys, handoffs, data and exceptions. For each credible product direction, connect the problem and expected value to supplied or researched evidence, compare decisive trade-offs, and identify an assumption that could invalidate the recommendation. Include current tools or doing nothing when viable. Do not treat a list of competitors as proof of demand or turn an untested hypothesis into a user need.

Recommend a first learning or delivery slice and explain what it will test, what it cannot establish, which roles and invariants it preserves, and what is deferred. Link it to the wider product trajectory without specifying every future feature. Selection of a journey does not also approve dropping accounts, changing the audience or abandoning other requested capabilities. Keep substantive product reductions separate from technical choices in the decision exchange.

## Adapt the conversation, preserve the reasoning

For a novice, explain consequences through concrete journeys and recommend options in familiar language. For an expert, expose assumptions, constraints, evidence and trade-offs at the relevant technical depth; assess their alternatives on the same criteria. Neither expertise nor delegation is a reason to simplify away the requested ambition. Avoid teaching fundamentals already demonstrated by the user. Use explicit delegated choices without repeated confirmation, and keep undecided commitments provisional.

## Judge readiness by substance

Before handing work to a dependent stage, check the actual decision and evidence, not the presence of a document:

- Exploration: enough evidence and uncertainty analysis to compare directions; remaining field research and its decision impact are explicit.
- Framing: actor journeys, invariants, exclusions and observable acceptance are coherent with the vision; material unresolved behavior blocks only its dependent implementation.
- Design: resolve design-to-code; record alternatives, selected master, derived screens and prototype/verification separately. Selecting a direction does not complete the remaining requested design scope.
- Architecture and data: resolve decision-architecture; connect boundaries and constraints to the journeys, failure cases, migration and operating conditions that justify them. A diagram or database name is not the decision's evidence.
- Planning and delivery: each slice has a user outcome and evidence that could fail when that outcome is violated. Integration must preserve shared contracts and existing decisions.

Keep these checks in existing owners. Evidence may be an explicit decision, a reviewed model, a focused experiment or an executed test depending on the stage; not every discovery hypothesis is executable. Report missing evidence as unknown or blocked. Continue independent authorized work rather than imposing a ceremonial approval or generating a dossier for each discipline.
