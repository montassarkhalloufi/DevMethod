# DevMethod opportunity research — 2026-09-16

Independent, bounded primary-source review. Sources opened on 2026-09-16. No repository edits, native model call, participant recruitment, or reopening of stopped campaigns. This is opportunity evidence and a proposed experiment, not a demonstrated breakthrough or market-gap estimate.

## Decision for the prototype

Prioritize **discovering and preserving product intent through experienced consequences**. A person should be able to try materially different ways a product could work, explain which consequence matters, change their mind, and continue building without losing that reason. This is an inference from the evidence below, not a source's demonstrated solution.

The prospective difference from DevMethod's existing guidance is an interaction and executable product experience, not another instruction to explore alternatives, write an ADR, or preserve context. Those procedures already exist. A canvas with chat, annotations, variants, and decision cards alone does not establish novelty.

## Evidence and counterevidence

| Primary source and date | What it supports | Boundary / contrary evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Li et al., Vibe Coding in Product Teams, v3, 2026-05-01](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10652v3) | Interviews with 22 product-team practitioners describe faster prototyping and broader participation, alongside tension between quickly realizing a design and reflecting on the underlying intention. Integration, fine control and responsibility also matter. | Interviews occurred **May–June 2025**, not 2026. Qualitative reports across heterogeneous roles cannot estimate prevalence or current tool failure. Participants also report greater creativity and autonomy. The paper identifies a tension, not evidence that more process solves it. |
| [Sarkar & Drosos, PPIG 2025, revision 2025-10-03](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23253v2) | Roughly 8.5 hours of selected public sessions show iterative prompting, inspection and manual changes. Expertise moves toward context management and choosing when to intervene. | Small, curated, performative video corpus from an emerging practice. It does not establish that users want a new workflow product, or that every intervention is waste. |
| [Hu et al., Why Human Guidance Matters, v2, 2026-03-06](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10473v2) | Twenty experiments involving 737 participants support retaining human direction rather than assuming fully AI-directed iteration improves indefinitely. Human instruction with AI evaluation performed well in the studied setting. | Task: reproduce ten animal reference images through SVG, largely using GPT-5 and linear iteration. This is **not** 737 people building real applications. Authors explicitly call for website/game studies; specialized models may reduce the gap. Do not infer a universal allocation of authority. |
| [Microsoft Research, Promptions, 2025-12-10](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/promptions-helps-make-ai-prompting-more-precise-with-dynamic-ui-controls/) | A formative study with 38 professionals and a comparison with 16 technical professionals suggest contextual controls can reduce repeated phrasing and support reflection. | Comprehension tasks, not product creation. Some controls were harder to interpret because their effects appeared only afterward. Dynamic options already exist; adding more knobs may increase friction. |
| [Chen et al., SpecifyUI, 2025-09-09](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07334) | Structured, hierarchical UI intent and targeted edits are existing research directions. The authors report better alignment/control than their Stitch baseline in a 16-designer study. | A preprint and bounded comparison against a then-current baseline, not all tools. Strong prior art against claiming that structured intent plus visual editing itself is new. |
| [Figma, properties panel and annotations, 2026-07-30](https://www.figma.com/blog/properties-panel-and-annotations-now-in-figma-make/) | Current product documentation describes contextual annotations, direct property changes, staged review and code updates. | Vendor account establishes offered functionality, not independent benefit. Its token/speed claims are not adopted here. Properties initially apply to new Make designs. Nevertheless, “point at UI and guide an agent” is plainly not an unmet category. |
| [Wadinambiarachchi et al., CHI 2024, 2024-03-17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11164) | A between-participant visual-ideation experiment (N=60) found greater fixation and fewer, less varied/original ideas with the studied AI image generator. | Older models and visual ideation, not agent-built products. Useful as a failure mode to probe, not evidence that current AI suppresses all creativity. It challenges indiscriminate generation of many similar alternatives. |
| [METR, study-design update, 2026-02-24](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) | Measurement must include actual work and selection effects. METR says its later results are unreliable for estimating current productivity because participation, task selection and concurrent-agent time tracking changed. | The earlier 19% slowdown must not become a current universal claim. Later raw estimates suggest speedup but have substantial uncertainty and selection bias. Faster-feeling prototypes and self-reported savings do not prove total value. |

None of these sources establishes market size, willingness to pay, or the absence of an adequate existing solution. No competitor execution was conducted. No finding depends on a handful of online complaints.

## Three genuinely different conceptions, plus the simple comparator

| Conception | User problem and actual interaction | Mechanism to isolate / main reason it could lose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Simple comparator: conversation + two ordinary prototypes + one-page choice note** | User tries alternatives, tells the agent what matters, saves a short brief and builds. This is a strong available workflow, not a deliberately weak baseline. | May already solve the need with less interface/setup. Give it the same options, information, time and ability to edit/revert. |
| **Experience rehearsal — preferred** | User enters a realistic situation in two materially different product concepts, sees the consequences, and chooses or rejects them. Decisions remain linked to the concrete interaction where the preference arose. | Externalizes tacit intent through action. Must improve comprehension or change quality beyond showing the same scenarios in ordinary prototypes. Cosmetic alternatives, deterministic theater or extra information would not demonstrate this mechanism. |
| **Editable product contract** | User adjusts a policy (who may confirm, what is remembered, what happens offline), sees linked journeys and architecture consequences, then accepts a scoped change while preserving other choices. | Makes decision dependencies manipulable across design and implementation. Risk: a new specification editor and maintenance burden. Existing ADRs plus clear agent instructions may suffice. A link graph alone has no demonstrated benefit. |
| **Negotiated delegation** | User specifies outcomes and boundaries, lets the agent choose reversible details, and receives only proposals that cross a meaningful boundary, with a preview and undo. | Allocates attention to decisions that matter rather than approvals for every step. Risk: interruption/control interfaces already common, inaccurate boundary inference, and excessive oversight. Requires actual agent work before claiming benefit; a scripted UI alone cannot establish it. |

These may compose later, but building all three now would prevent learning which mechanism matters. Keep the preferred prototype narrow and usable; do not replace the whole development environment.

## Smallest useful experience to build now

Use one openly fictional product, for example a community room-booking service. Compare **immediate reservation with a waitlist** versus **preference collection followed by organizer allocation**. These differ in user behavior, authority, data and coordination, rather than color/layout. Let the user actually request a slot, encounter a conflict, change a request and see the other person's outcome. Show clearly which behavior is simulated; no invented adoption, revenue or reliability predictions.

Keep all options editable. Let the user record “why this consequence matters,” reject both concepts, change a previous choice, and export a concise build brief plus a runnable chosen slice. On changing the policy, show which accepted experience changes and which is preserved. Preserve visual identity across the alternatives so visual polish does not silently decide the policy choice. Architectural consequences should be expressed as visible user effects, such as whether a booking survives another device or whether a coordinator must approve it.

An authored, offline rehearsal can prove the interaction works. It cannot prove arbitrary app generation, correct architectural inference, autonomous implementation or real-user value. Label those boundaries in the experience and report, rather than disguising the fixed scenario as live intelligence.

## Experiment that can reject the preferred conception

1. First complete an end-to-end scripted walkthrough of both rehearsal and simple comparator with identical scenarios. This demonstrates usability of the artifact only. Record authorship and interventions; do not substitute an agent persona for a participant.
2. For a later authorized formative study, use a counterbalanced within-person comparison on two comparable product briefs, with actual target creators (for example 6–8 product/design/technical leads). This sample is for discovering mechanism failures, not causal population estimates. Let participants supply a real preference before the tool suggests options.
3. Give both conditions identical options and consequence information. Change one requirement after an initial decision; inspect whether the participant can explain the trade-off, preserve the still-valid choice and revise the relevant experience. Measure active human time, misunderstood consequences, unwanted changes, reversals and setup effort. Confidence/satisfaction is secondary.
4. Predeclare the decision: retain the rehearsal only if a specific observed failure of the simple comparator is resolved without a material extra burden. If the same information in ordinary prototypes works equally well, use that. If users simply prefer more polish, it is not evidence for a reasoning mechanism. If neither condition helps users discover a consequential preference, revisit the problem instead of adding process.

The intended user benefit is better product judgment and continuity of that judgment. The research provides reasons to investigate it; the prototype and future observations must earn the claim.
