# Explain and compare design choices

Use during open visual exploration or a requested evidence review. Keep the reasoning proportional to the decision; use the existing design record. Research guides a hypothesis; it does not predict a product's winning design by itself.

## Targeted collection and synthesis

For an open direction, collect evidence before creating alternatives; use the research step in [visual creation](visual-creation.md). Select sources for the decisions at hand, not to fill a fixed citation quota. Stop when the material design questions have usable support or explicit unknowns; do not turn a bounded design task into exhaustive research.

- Visual references: inspect the original product or creator's work. Record publication date when available, consultation date, market and medium. A showcase or vendor trend report is inspiration, not evidence of adoption or task effectiveness. Do not describe one example as an industry-wide trend.
- Ergonomics and perception: consult official standards and original studies or research syntheses. Check the studied audience, task, method, outcome and limitations before transferring a finding. Conflicting or weak evidence remains visible; do not cherry-pick a study to justify a predetermined design. An accessibility requirement and an empirical preference result carry different authority.
- Target users: reuse available observations with their context and sample limitations. Competitor reviews are indirect evidence, not observations of this product. Explicitly record when target-user evidence is absent; agent inspection or invented personas do not supply it.

For each source used, keep a compact identifier/link, provenance, publication date if known, consultation date, relevant finding, proposed application and transfer limits in the existing design record. Unknown dates stay unknown. Refresh time-sensitive claims when relevant; do not repeat unchanged research by ritual.

Summarize what to retain, explore, enforce and test before rendering. Carry the important hypotheses into prototype verification with an observable task and a criterion that could disconfirm the expected benefit. User selection establishes the visual direction, not measured usability or population preference.

## Evidence types

| Type | What to record | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | Exact criterion, affected element, measurement and pass/fail or unchecked | Aesthetic preference or full conformance from one check |
| Empirical research | Primary source, date, studied task/audience, result, limits and proposed application | That the result transfers to this audience or palette |
| Design heuristic | Rule, visible application, expected benefit and trade-off | A scientific law or guaranteed conversion gain |
| Trend observation | Dated original source, market/medium, observed style and relevance | Universal taste, accessibility or superior usability |
| Product hypothesis | Proposed direction, target audience, reason and test that could reject it | A measured result before testing |

Search current sources when claiming a style or color is fashionable. Read primary research before attributing findings. If browsing or a source is unavailable, mark the claim unverified and continue independent design work. Do not invent citations, preference percentages, participants or conversion estimates. Avoid universal color psychology claims such as “green creates trust.”
